Pooled across 14 studies measuring both outcome types in the same participants (van den Berg, Hartig, & Staats, 2015). Range reflects heterogeneity in stressor type and exposure duration, not a single CI.

The sharpest empirical evidence on SRT–ART divergence comes from studies that measure both theories' signature outcomes within the same participants and exposure conditions. After an identifiable acute stressor, nature-exposed participants show larger effects on autonomic outcomes — cardiovascular return-to-baseline, cortisol suppression, skin-conductance recovery — than on the directed-attention outcomes ART predicts: backwards digit-span, restorativeness scales, sustained-attention tasks.

The original demonstration (Ulrich et al., 1991) showed recovery within four to seven minutes of nature exposure post-surgery — a timescale consistent with SRT's parasympathetic rebound account. Directed-attention recovery, as Kaplan (1995) models it, operates on a longer timescale and does not require a prior stressor as a precondition.

What the data does not show. The smaller attentional effect is still real — ART's mechanism is not absent. The data also does not show whether the gap is a genuine theoretical difference or an artefact of differential measurement sensitivity. These two interpretations are not yet distinguishable from pooled data.

Sources. Ulrich et al. (1991), Environment & Behavior; Kaplan (1995), Environment & Behavior; van den Berg, Hartig, & Staats (2015); Laumann, Gärling, & Stormark (2003).
Warrant · Step 2
Two theories with overlapping scope can only be discriminated by a design that independently activates each mechanism.
SRT
autonomic · stress-axis
stressor→SAM axis→parasympathetic rebound→HPA suppression
ART
attentional · resource
mental fatigue→directed-attention depleted→soft fascination engages→capacity restored

The argument from differential effect sizes to a theoretical verdict rests on a logical principle: a larger effect size for one theory's outcomes is only evidence of that theory's priority if the design independently activates each mechanism. SRT and ART describe genuinely distinct causal pathways — one runs through the body's acute stress axis, the other through attentional resource allocation. They carry different predictions: SRT predicts faster physiological recovery when a prior stressor is present; ART predicts directed-attention improvement whether or not a prior stressor occurred.

But in virtually every published study, both pathways are simultaneously active: participants are both post-stressor and attention-fatigued, and the natural environments used are simultaneously low-threat and low-demand. The design conflates activation conditions for both theories.

Backing. Woodward (2003), Making Things Happen (interventionist causation); Hartig (2021), Handbook of Environmental Psychology; Ulrich (1983), Science.
Claim · Step 3
The frameworks are not yet competitive — each leads on a different sub-process.
SRT leads on…
Acute recovery from an identifiable stressor. Direct physiological evidence, specified mechanism, matched timescale (minutes).
ART leads on…
Sustained directed-attention restoration over longer exposures. Larger evidence base, smaller effects, harder-to-operationalise mechanism.
Qualifier. No published study has run a clean crossed design holding stressor presence and environment demand-level independent while measuring both outcome types with matched sensitivity. Until that design exists, "SRT wins on the acute trajectory" is defensible but not definitive.

Reading the differential effect-size data through the warrant produces a two-part qualified claim rather than a single winner. SRT carries the stronger warrant for acute recovery from an identifiable stressor — the physiological evidence is direct, the mechanism (SAM-axis rebound via parasympathetic activation) is specified to a timescale consistent with the data (minutes, not hours), and the designs that best match SRT's predictions show the larger effects.

ART carries the stronger warrant for directed-attention restoration over longer exposures without a mandatory prior stressor — its evidence base is larger, but effect sizes are smaller and the mechanism (involuntary attention engagement replenishing a depletable resource) is harder to operationalise independently of the outcome it predicts.

This is the claim a journey can defensibly leave a reader with in 2026. It is stronger than a "both explain the same thing" dismissal; it is weaker than a decisive theoretical verdict. Both frameworks survive, but for different aspects of the restorative response.

Rebuttal · Step 4 · the defeater that lives in the room
The effect-size gap may be a measurement artefact, not a theoretical discriminant.
a
Genuine mechanistic difference. SRT's mechanism is more potent for acute recovery.
supports the claim
b
Differential measurement sensitivity. Autonomic instruments simply detect more than behavioural attention tests.
defeats the claim
c
Shared upstream pathway. Affect mediates both — the gap reflects how quickly affect feeds back into each system.
defeats the claim

The claim above treats the differential effect-size pattern as evidence that SRT's mechanism is more potent for acute recovery. A coherent rebuttal exists. If the instruments used to measure SRT outcomes — continuous cardiovascular monitoring, salivary cortisol assay, galvanic skin response — are simply more sensitive and more directly tied to the exposure manipulation than those used for ART outcomes — backwards digit span, self-reported restorativeness, selective attention tasks — then the larger effect size for SRT outcomes would appear even if both mechanisms operated at identical strength. On this reading, the divergence tells us about the psychometrics of the measurement tools, not about the theoretical priority of either framework.

Settling this would require a study that holds measurement sensitivity constant across both outcome domains — for instance, using resting-state fMRI connectivity as the outcome for both (default-mode network for ART-aligned restoration; heart-rate variability with neural correlates for SRT-aligned recovery). The field has begun moving in this direction (Bratman et al., 2019), but crossed designs at scale have not yet been run.

Defeater sources. Hartig, Evans, Jamner, Davis, & Gärling (2003); Joye & van den Berg (2011), Psychological Bulletin; Bratman, Anderson, Berman et al. (2019), Science Advances.
