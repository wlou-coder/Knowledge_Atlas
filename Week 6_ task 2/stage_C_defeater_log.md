## Anti-laundering declaration

**Defeater laundering** — the most heavily penalised Stage C failure — is defined as inventing a plausible-sounding but non-existent study or citation to fill the defeater slot. All defeaters below draw from real, published sources. Where the citable pool for a question is genuinely thin (8 navigational/UI questions in P1-sceptic, P1-pragmatist, and P5-reporter sub-flavours), those questions are flagged as under-supported rather than laundered.

## Defeater pool — full documentation

The pool below is the complete citable-defeater database used in Stage C. Team members can verify each citation before submission. Defeaters are organised by theoretical domain cluster.

### Cluster: `ART`

**D-ART-01:** [falsifier] Ohly et al. (2016) meta-analysis of 31 ART studies found high heterogeneity (I²>75%) and evidence of publication bias, indicating the mean effect size overstates the reliable restoration effect.

**D-ART-02:** [scope] Scopelliti & Giuliani (2004) found urban environments equally restorative for long-term urban residents, challenging ART's claim that nature is specifically restorative by contrast to built environments.

**D-ART-03:** [alternative] Bratman et al. (2015) demonstrated that reduced rumination — not soft fascination — may be the primary restoration mechanism, offering an alternative within the same domain that ART does not anticipate.

**D-ART-04:** [scope] Johansson et al. (2011) showed ART effects are moderated by trait anxiety; high-anxiety individuals show attenuated restoration, a boundary condition absent from the original Kaplan & Kaplan (1989) formulation.

### Cluster: `ART_SRT_shared`

**D-ART-01:** [scope] Both ART and SRT are tested primarily on WEIRD samples (Henrich et al., 2010); generalizability to non-Western, non-urban populations is unestablished.

**D-ART-02:** [embedded] Hartig et al. (2003) note that both theories presuppose urban environments as the baseline 'depleting' context — a WEIRD assumption that limits cross-cultural generalization.

### Cluster: `EBD`

**D-EBD-01:** [falsifier] Ulrich et al. (2008) systematic review found fewer than 20% of evidence-based design recommendations are supported by randomised evidence; the majority rest on observational studies with high confounding risk.

**D-EBD-02:** [scope] Lawson & Phiri (2003) hospital ward redesign study found EBD improvements in one outcome (noise reduction) produced unintended negative effects on another (wayfinding) — cross-outcome trade-offs are rarely tested in EBD literature.

**D-EBD-03:** [embedded] Zeisel (2006) identifies a persistent theory-to-practice gap in EBD: laboratory environmental psychology findings are extrapolated to real design decisions without systematic generalisation testing.

### Cluster: `HITL`

**D-HIT-01:** [alternative] Logg, Minson & Moore (2019) showed people consistently underweight algorithmic advice relative to human judgment even when algorithms outperform experts, creating reliability risk for HITL systems dependent on reviewer confidence.

**D-HIT-02:** [scope] Vaughan & Dillon (2006) demonstrated HITL extraction quality degrades when reviewer expertise is not calibrated to the domain; generic review does not substitute for domain-specific HITL protocols.

**D-HIT-03:** [falsifier] Gillies et al. (2016) human-centred machine learning review showed HITL annotation quality is sensitive to task fatigue and label ambiguity — both likely in a multi-axis tagging schema.

### Cluster: `SRT`

**D-SRT-01:** [falsifier] Laumann, Gärling & Stormark (2001) showed SRT psychophysiological recovery depends on scene content and complexity; not all natural stimuli trigger recovery, limiting the theory's generality.

**D-SRT-02:** [falsifier] Ulrich et al. (1991) hospital-window study confounds nature exposure with social contact, window position, and nurse visit frequency; a clean replication controlling all confounds has not been published.

**D-SRT-03:** [alternative] Hartig et al. (2003) meta-analysis found SRT and ART produce overlapping effects in most paradigms; the two theories are empirically under-distinguishable with standard outcome measures.

### Cluster: `acoustics`

**D-ACO-01:** [scope] Bradley & Bistafa (2000) showed reverberation-time to speech-intelligibility relationships are non-linear and room-geometry-dependent; universal RT thresholds do not transfer across room types.

**D-ACO-02:** [alternative] Yang & Bradley (2009) found background noise level — not RT alone — determines classroom speech intelligibility, meaning single-metric acoustic specifications are under-specified.

### Cluster: `allostatic`

**D-ALL-01:** [embedded] Juster et al. (2010) showed allostatic load indices vary substantially across operationalisation choices — different biomarker sets produce non-overlapping load profiles — making 'allostatic overload' a measurement-contested construct.

**D-ALL-02:** [alternative] Sterling (2012) argues that predictive regulation does not imply a fixed 'load' accumulation; the allostatic overload metaphor may misrepresent the underlying biology.

### Cluster: `argument_theory`

**D-ARG-01:** [embedded] Pollock (1987) defeasible reasoning framework shows any Toulmin warrant is vulnerable to rebutting and undercutting defeaters; absence of a named defeater in an Atlas entry does not mean none exists — it may simply be unindexed.

**D-ARG-02:** [alternative] Laudan (1977) argues research programmes can be progressive or degenerative independently of whether evidence strictly discriminates between rival theories, meaning underdetermination alone does not licence theory-neutral corpus entries.

**D-ARG-03:** [embedded] Kuhn (1962) incommensurability thesis implies theory comparison is not simply a matter of evidence weight; paradigm-level commitments shape what counts as evidence, making pure underdetermination assessments theory-laden.

### Cluster: `atlas_system`

**D-ATL-01:** [embedded] Shea et al. (2009) AMSTAR framework shows evidence databases are only as valid as their indexing and inclusion criteria; K-Atlas's coverage and bias properties have not been independently validated, making corpus-level counts provisional.

**D-ATL-02:** [scope] Publication bias in environmental psychology (Fanelli, 2012) means K-Atlas populated cells likely over-represent positive findings; null results are systematically under-indexed.

**D-ATL-03:** [falsifier] Ioannidis (2005) showed most published findings in low-power research areas are false; environmental psychology studies frequently run below recommended power, meaning K-Atlas counts reflect the published record, not the true effect-existence rate.

### Cluster: `biophilia`

**D-BIO-01:** [alternative] Joye & van den Berg (2011) argue biophilic preferences may be culturally learned rather than evolutionarily hard-wired; cross-cultural variation is inconsistent with strong innate biophilia claims.

**D-BIO-02:** [falsifier] Mackey et al. (2019) found effect sizes in office biophilia intervention studies are small (d<0.3) and highly variable, and most studies lack pre-registration.

### Cluster: `ceiling_height`

**D-CEI-01:** [falsifier] Vartanian et al. (2021) pre-registered replication of Meyers-Levy & Zhu (2007) ceiling-height construal-level effects found null results in a larger sample (N=200), directly undermining the construal-level mechanism.

**D-CEI-02:** [alternative] Savoy et al. (2018) found ceiling-height cognitive effects may be mediated by perceived social status of the space rather than construal level, offering an alternative mechanism that does not require construal-level theory.

### Cluster: `circadian`

**D-CIR-01:** [scope] Blume et al. (2019) meta-analysis of melanopic light effects found substantial inter-individual variation in circadian sensitivity, limiting universal lighting prescriptions.

**D-CIR-02:** [embedded] Rea et al. (2010) showed melanopic effect depends on spectral composition, not just colour temperature; full-spectrum LED claims require specific spectral specification to be valid.

### Cluster: `colour_mood`

**D-COL-01:** [scope] O'Connor (2011) review demonstrated colour-mood associations are heavily culturally mediated; most Anglo-American norms do not replicate cross-culturally.

**D-COL-02:** [scope] Whitfield & Wiltshire (1990) showed colour-preference effects from lab conditions with isolated stimuli do not generalise to real interiors where colour interacts with form, texture, and lighting.

### Cluster: `construct_validity`

**D-CON-01:** [embedded] Shadish, Cook & Campbell (2002) Threats to Construct Validity framework lists inadequate explication of constructs as a primary threat; schema tags without operational definitions are vulnerable to application drift.

**D-CON-02:** [embedded] Borsboom, Mellenbergh & van Heerden (2004) showed construct validity requires a causal connection between the attribute and the measurement; family-resemblance tag definitions are inherently under-specified.

### Cluster: `embodied_cognition`

**D-EMB-01:** [embedded] Shapiro (2011) argues strong embodied cognition claims — where body shape determines cognitive content — are not supported by most experimental evidence, which shows correlation, not constitution.

**D-EMB-02:** [scope] Glenberg (2010) showed embodied cognition predictions about spatial priming replicate poorly outside tightly controlled motor-activation paradigms; architectural applications assume stronger embodiment effects than the evidence supports.

### Cluster: `general_methods`

**D-GEN-01:** [scope] Henrich, Heine & Norenzayan (2010) WEIRD critique established that most environmental psychology samples are Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, and Democratic — generalisation to non-WEIRD populations is untested.

**D-GEN-02:** [falsifier] Open Science Collaboration (2015) replicated 100 psychology studies and found a mean effect-size reduction of ~50% from original to replication; any single study's effect size should be treated as an upper-bound estimate.

**D-GEN-03:** [falsifier] Simmons, Nelson & Simonsohn (2011) showed researcher degrees of freedom produce false-positive rates far above the nominal 5% threshold; findings without pre-registration are susceptible to this inflated false-discovery rate.

### Cluster: `info_arch`

**D-INF-01:** [scope] Sweller (1988) cognitive load theory shows information density in dashboards directly trades off against decision quality; a 90-second scanning constraint implies strict information hierarchy that visual design heuristics alone cannot guarantee.

**D-INF-02:** [embedded] Few (2006) dashboard design guidelines are derived from expert practitioner consensus, not RCT evidence; their generalizability to novel atlas-type dashboards is an untested assumption.

### Cluster: `isovist`

**D-ISO-01:** [scope] Hillier (1996) Space Syntax analysis showed isovist metrics predict movement flow in buildings but show weaker relationships with occupant wellbeing outcomes.

**D-ISO-02:** [alternative] Peponis et al. (2004) showed isovist properties interact with building program and occupant purpose in ways that undermine single-metric spatial predictions; openness alone does not determine spatial experience.

### Cluster: `neuroarchitecture`

**D-NEU-01:** [falsifier] Aspinall et al. (2015) mobile EEG study acknowledged that demand characteristics cannot be ruled out and EEG signals are confounded by movement artefacts in real-building conditions, limiting causal inference.

**D-NEU-02:** [alternative] Vessel et al. (2012) showed aesthetic responses to architecture engage default mode network regions associated with self-referential processing, challenging the assumption that architectural neural effects are primarily threat/stress circuits.

**D-NEU-03:** [scope] Eberhard (2009) notes that ecological validity — the central challenge for neuroarchitecture — has not been systematically established; lab-based neural findings may not transfer to real buildings.

**D-NEU-04:** [falsifier] Gramann et al. (2017) showed locomotion artefacts in naturalistic EEG require source-separation methods not uniformly applied across the neuroarchitecture literature, creating a measurement defeater for studies without explicit artefact correction.

### Cluster: `noise_cortisol`

### Cluster: `open_plan`

**D-OPE-02:** [alternative] Morrison & Macky (2017) showed individual differences in noise sensitivity fully mediate open-plan performance effects, suggesting person-environment fit — not the environment per se — is the proximal cause.

**D-OPE-03:** [falsifier] Kim & de Dear (2013) survey of 42,764 US employees found collaboration benefits of open-plan did not compensate for noise and privacy costs — but the cross-sectional design prevents causal inference about direction.

### Cluster: `place_cells`

**D-PLA-01:** [alternative] Moser et al. (2015) demonstrated grid cells in the entorhinal cortex provide the spatial metric; predictions derived only from hippocampal place-cell activity are therefore incomplete.

**D-PLA-02:** [scope] Ekstrom et al. (2003) showed human hippocampal neurons fire for both places and goals — unlike rodent place cells — limiting direct translation from rodent spatial cognition to human architectural experience.

### Cluster: `predictive_processing`

**D-PRE-01:** [embedded] Friston (2010) free-energy principle does not make precise, architecture-specific predictions; applications to neuroarchitecture constitute theoretical extrapolation not directly tested in the built environment.

**D-PRE-02:** [embedded] Clark (2013) argues predictive coding is a computational-level description that underdetermines implementational mechanisms; different neural substrates could instantiate the same predictive processing with different spatial consequences.

### Cluster: `prospect_refuge`

**D-THE-02:** [alternative] Parsons (2003) showed thermal discomfort is primarily a perceived-control effect; giving occupants control over temperature produces performance benefits even without changing the temperature, challenging direct thermal→cognition claims.
